Frequently Asked Questions

Honest answers to the most common questions about owning an EV without a private garage.

A garage orphan is anyone who owns an EV (or wants to buy one) but lives in an apartment, condo, townhome, or urban house without a private driveway or dedicated electrical outlet.

Over 40% of American households fall into this group. You don't need a single-family home with a two-car garage to drive electric—you just charge a little differently: plugging into standard wall outlets overnight, charging while grocery shopping or working, using public fast chargers, or sharing driveways with neighbors.

In more than 18 states (including California, Colorado, Florida, Hawaii, New York, New Jersey, Oregon, Virginia, and Washington), state law explicitly makes it illegal for an HOA, condo board, or landlord to unreasonably deny a resident's request to install an EV charger at their assigned parking stall.

As long as you agree to pay for the installation and your own electricity, the board cannot put up arbitrary roadblocks. Our Free Proposal Kit gives you the exact legal clause to include in your letter.

Yes, absolutely. This is called Level 1 charging. It's slower (giving you about 3 to 5 miles of driving range per hour plugged in), but if you plug in for 12 hours overnight, you wake up with 36 to 60 miles of fresh range.

Since the average American drives around 30 miles per day, a normal wall outlet covers 100% of daily driving for most commuters. Just be sure to use a thick, commercial 10-gauge outdoor extension cord rather than a thin household cord.

Think of it like an "Airbnb for EV chargers." Platforms like EVmatch and neighborhood co-ops allow homeowners who have private driveways and 240V Level 2 chargers to rent them out to nearby apartment dwellers.

If you live in an apartment, you can reserve a spot in a neighbor's driveway down the street for a few hours, get a fast full charge at low home-electricity rates, and pick up your car in the morning.

Modern EVs have active liquid cooling systems designed to protect the battery during fast charging. However, two simple habits will keep your battery healthy for years:

  • Unplug at 80%: The last 20% charges much slower and creates the most heat.
  • Mix in slower charging: Take advantage of slower Level 2 public chargers whenever you're at the gym, grocery store, or movies.
